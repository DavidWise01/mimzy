The Symbiont

the ternary clock addresses the deck · 1 color-bit + 3 trits · the gate halts the clock

Two machines that need each other. The 27-step ternary odometer (three diode-ternary timers, nested 1:3:9) is the clock — it counts the low address in trits. Add the color bit on top (which is also the high bit of the opcode group) and 27 × 2 = 54 = 52 cards + 2 jokers: the deck is the memory it addresses. Each card the clock lands on decodes to a 6-bit instruction; the world-touching SYS cards and the jokers HALT the clock at the gate. The clock drives the deck; the deck, through its gate, drives the clock back. Symbiosis.

the symbiosis: the address is 1 color-bit + 3 trits. The three ternary timers (C low ×1, B mid ÷3, A high ÷9) count the 27 cells of a page; the color bit is the high bit that selects the page — and it is the very same bit that tops the 2-bit opcode group, so red ⇒ suits 00/01 (♥ ALU · ♦ MOVE) and black ⇒ suits 10/11 (♠ FLOW · ♣ SYS). Each page's home cell (000) is its joker — the self-dual centre of the 27-lattice, the wild/witness; the other 26 cells are 2 suits × 13 ranks. The card decodes to its 6-bit word (suit<<4 | rank); internal ops fire freely, but ♣ SYS writes and the jokers HALT — the gate stops the clock until you confirm. So the clock drives the deck, and the deck's gate drives the clock: neither runs alone. Honest seam (unchanged): color and suit are powers of 2 — clean; rank is 13, prime — it never tiles the trits or the nibble. A container fused to a clock, not an isomorphism.
